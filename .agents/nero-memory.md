## project-longlife-citation-integrity

*LongLife generation collapses distinct same-journal studies under one [n] and carries placeholder/transposed sources; verify via NCBI eutils*

LongLife article generation has a recurring citation-integrity failure the structural
lint does NOT catch (it only checks `max([n]) <= len(sources)` and equal array lengths):

- **Collapse:** when several distinct studies share a journal name (e.g. many *Body Image*
  papers), the model reuses ONE number for all of them. On the beauty material `[4]` alone
  covered five different studies, `[1]` two Becker papers, `[9]` two meta-analyses. Reader
  clicks `[4]` five times, lands on one URL = credibility death.
- **Placeholders/transposition:** older posts carry `source_names: ""` and
  `source_descriptions: "Джерело не вказане"`, and at least one real transposition (PMID
  38850713 was labelled *Maternal & Child Nutrition* but is actually Ladwig 2024).
- **Phantom numbers in prose:** a specific stat can be invented. The beauty post claimed
  "OR 1.78 (95% CI 1.26-2.49)"; the actual Stice 2021 meta reports OR 1.64 (1.09-2.46).

**Why:** the blog's whole value is "reads the actual literature, every claim sourced". A
wrong CI or a collapsed citation is exactly what the secondary audience (dietitians/trainers)
catches. This is not cosmetic.

**How to apply:** when polishing or QA-ing a LongLife post, treat one number = one paper.
Split collapsed `[n]`, recover the real URL per study, and VERIFY titles via NCBI eutils
(bot-friendly, no captcha): `esummary.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=<comma-ids>&retmode=json`. curl to
eutils failed empty in the sandbox once; WebFetch on the same URL worked. For liveness,
treat 401/403/405/429 as "URL valid" (academic publishers + france24 + jamanetwork
hard-block bots) and only 404/410/DNS as broken. See [[longlife-faion-net]].
